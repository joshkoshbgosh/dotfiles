Good — these three are all the same underlying question: *what does org already record natively, and what can supertag's query layer actually see?* That distinction drives everything.

## 1. Native repeaters: how they work

A routine is a heading with a repeating timestamp:

```org
* TODO Morning pages
  SCHEDULED: <2026-09-16 Wed +1d>
```

When you mark it DONE, org automatically shifts `SCHEDULED` forward one day and flips it back to TODO. Three repeater flavors:
- `+1d` — next occurrence is tomorrow *from today* (miss a day, no backlog)
- `++1d` — catch-up style; missed days queue up as overdue
- `.+1d` — next occurrence is one day *from when you completed it* (interval anchored to completion)

For routines, `+1d` is almost always right — you don't want three overdue "morning pages" stacking up.

**History, honestly:** by default only the *latest* `CLOSED:` timestamp survives — each completion overwrites it. So "show me all completions in September" is **not** available out of the box. To keep per-occurrence history you opt into logging: `org-log-repeat` set to `time` or `note` appends a timestamped entry to the `LOGBOOK` drawer on every repeat, accumulating a full history under the heading. That history is visible and greppable, and org-ql/agenda can query `CLOSED`/logbook ranges — but supertag's query grammar indexes node fields and TODO states, *not* drawer contents. So: history = yes, preserved in the file; history in a *supertag saved query or table view* = no, without verification work on my part. Flagging that as the one thing I'd test before committing to repeaters.

## 2. The org-native habit approach: `org-habit`

Habits are a thin layer on repeaters. A habit is a TODO with a `STYLE: habit` property and typically a `.+` repeater:

```org
* TODO Exercise
  SCHEDULED: <2026-09-16 Wed .+1d>
  :PROPERTIES:
  :STYLE:    habit
  :END:
```

You enable the `org-habit` module, and the agenda shows a consistency graph — a row of `*`/`!` marks per day showing completions at a glance. Logging = marking DONE, same gesture as everything else; `target_per_week` becomes "how many `*`s do I expect," judged by eye on the graph rather than a computed field. This is the org-native answer: **no counter field, no log nodes — the graph *is* the tracking UI.** Its weakness is the same as repeaters: the graph lives in the agenda, not in supertag views, and streak math ("current streak: 6") isn't a stored value anywhere unless we compute it with a helper.

Given that, my recommendation for habits: single node per habit + `STYLE: habit` + `.+` repeater + `last_done :timestamp` field (one field, maintained by a rule on DONE) so supertag views can at least sort/filter by recency. `target_per_week` stays as the declared cadence. `log_count`, `start_date`, `end_date` all go.

## 3. CLOCK: queryable, but not by supertag

This is the crux. CLOCK data is *not* opaque — org has two mature consumers:
- **Clocktables** (`org-clock-report`): per-day/per-task sums,任意 range, a keystroke away.
- **org-ql**: predicates over clocked ranges (`clocked :from ... :to ...`), so "all sessions this week" is answerable today.

What's *not* possible is seeing sessions through supertag's lens: saved queries, table views, kanban, and automation rules operate on node fields (`actual_minutes`), and drawer contents are invisible to them. So the question reframes cleanly: **are your time questions asked in the agenda (CLOCK suffices, nothing to build) or in supertag views (needs session data as fields/nodes)?**

My read: keep CLOCK as the capture mechanism (it's frictionless and mobile-compatible), keep `actual_minutes` as the rolled-up total supertag *can* see, and defer queryable sessions until you feel a concrete absence — e.g. the day you want a table of Tuesday's sessions and the clocktable isn't enough. That's a real need, but it's not today's need, and building session nodes now is schema we'd have to maintain for a hypothetical.
